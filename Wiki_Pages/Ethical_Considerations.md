# Ethical Consideration 
## Structured using the Ethical Disclaimer canvas from Ethics for Designers (Gispen, 2017). 

### 1. The Current Situation is...
##### Campus already produces small pockets of dead time such as waiting at a printer, in a kitchen queue, between classes, where students stand near strangers with no shared activity and no low-stakes reason to interact. 
##### Our probes and interviews showed real desire and openness for spontaneous, 'whimsical' connection, but no existing format on campus channels it. We want to turn that incidental proximity into an optional, anonymous invitation to connect, without requiring students to schedule or reveal their identity to find a sense of connection.

### 2. The Stakeholders are...
##### •	Students who choose to leave a message/drawing trail on the big canvas mural/whiteboard.
##### •	Students who spontaneously interact via two different tablets at the same time that take part in Link-Up (synchronous activity).
##### •	Students who view a tablet without contributing
##### •	Other people physically nearby a tablet who did not choose to take part (bystanders)
##### •	The university (facilities, campus safety, IT, cleaners that share this space with students)
##### •	Our design team, who carry responsibility for ethical design and moderation of interaction.


### 3. Our Design Intentions are...
#### What do you want to change in the current situation, why?
##### •	Users: any student on campus -> no login required/anonymous by default
##### •	Context: two fixed physical tablets at natural waiting spots publicly on campus (e.g. printer, kitchen).
##### •	Purpose: create low-effort, low-stakes, whimsical opportunities for connection between strangers. Asynchronous and distributed via the drawing trail, and synchronous and distributed via Link-Up (spontaneous draw session).


### 4. Unethical Situations with our design
#### What dark, wrong or weird situations can you come up with? What would be really wrong?
#### What happens when it is used over a longer period of time?
#### What if it is used by the ‘wrong’ people? Or shared by different people?
#### What if it is used in a different context? Or for a different purpose? Or combined with other technology?
#### Who can access it? What happens if people hack it?

#### Situation 1: 
##### A message trail or Link-Up entry which is meant to be “anonymous” is still tied to a persistent local device/ID saved to the database. Over time it could be potentially identified to a real person through drawing style, timing or location. This undermines our promise to maintain confidentiality. 
##### Action Plan: Rotate the local ID periodically instead of fixing it to a device or time. Ensure that the database is private and ID/timestamp/precise location not easily exposed.
#### Situation 2:
##### Anonymous drawing/messaging between strangers is used to harass, insult or target other students online. The shared mural committed from other students or drawings shared in the Link-up adds risk that misuse and miscommunication can occur. This creates an unsafe space for students to engage with others and trust in interacting with our application. 
##### Action Plan: Physically, during the prototype testing and user observation, enable the team to have passive oversight and moderation. Online, the team should maintain constant oversight to remove inappropriate postings and implement a visible, easy “report a concern” button on the interface. 
#### Situation 3:
##### During the Link-Up activity, two strangers co-create one drawing, here it is unclear who kept, deleted or later have it appear in the public archive. Drawing/Message entries can also become part of a location's permanent growing history that the original author can't remove once others have drawn. The ownership and control of joint creation and layered artefact creation can be unclear.
##### Action Plan: Give both participants independent, equal rights to save or delete their own copy, neither needs the other's permission. Students who have contributed to their entry in the layered drawing have the right to delete their own entry via contacting the team, even after later contributions are added.

### 5. We take responsibility for...
#### Which situations fall within the ethical scope of the project? Why? 
##### •	Moderating public/anonymous content before and after it is visible on the platform, and giving every user a one-tap report button and become moderators too. 
##### •	Making consent visible and implemented before user interaction and content produced via the tablet. 
##### •	Letting any contributor delete their own entry, and either 'Link-Up' participant remove their own copy and independent of the other party.
##### •	Keeping the core features usable at the tablet without other phones, apps, or account required.


### 5. We do not take responsibility for...
#### Which situations are outside the ethical scope of the project? Why?
##### Which might be someone else’s responsibility?
##### •	Guaranteeing no stranger will ever be rude or unkind physically during the leaving of a trail or Link-Up session. How users act in real-time cannot be pre-screened and determined. Responsibility lies in the university's security team in handling out of control physical situations and interactions. 
##### •	Guaranteeing perfect anonymity, distinct handwriting, phrasing and timing can still make a contributor identifiable to someone who knows them. Our team will disclose this limitation clearly. 
##### •	Physical security or vandalism of the iPad itself, the team will implement measures (location on, find my ready, disclaimers etc.) If damaged, responsibly is on the user who mistreats the property.
